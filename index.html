From project to credits

Three steps between the client signing and credits landing in your name. Identical for every partner regardless of seniority.

Step 1 · Splits & SCR

The code holder decides

When a project sells, revenue is split among partners on the code. The code holder proposes, everyone else negotiates. Splits are set at the start and can be revised ex-post. Your share becomes your SCR, Single Count Revenue. $2M of SCR means roughly $4M of sold work at 50% splits.

Step 2 · PVA

Profitability, not revenue

Assume a typical project, no contractors or pass-throughs, so invoiced fees equal net revenue, and that is what SCR measures here. PVA = net revenue, minus an 18% net revenue charge to the firm, minus execution charges, the team's time at internal cost rates × 50% (20% for DI/VP levels). So PVA margin = 82% minus execution charges over net revenue, with 82% as the ceiling. Historical quintiles run 55 / 50 / 45 / 37, pricing targets 36%, ~40% is a fair working number.

Step 3 · The 50/50

Half yours, half the pool's

PVA splits evenly. Half stays with you as DPC Development Credits, your personal credits. Half funds the pool as Franchise Impact Credits, which redistribute by seniority. Credits are an internal unit, not dollars. Nothing becomes money until the very end.

Surviving

What it takes to keep the seat, and the sensitivity that shows it. SCR means net revenue (no contractors). Everything here reprices live off the multiplier, FI level, pool and draw settings above.

Two floors decide whether you keep the seat

Floor 1, the flag. Total credits × multiplier falls short of the draw and the bonus goes negative. No bonus, a formal flag, a partner-level PIP, recover within 1 to 2 years or you are out. At baseline assumptions that is SCR below ~$735K.

Floor 2, the survival line. Your own generation, 100% of your credits × the multiplier with the pool excluded from both sides, must cover your draw. At 40% PVA in a 75% year that is ~$1.18M of SCR. Priced for a weak year, 30% PVA at 60%, it becomes the ~$2M partners quote.

Between the floors sits the trap zone, amber below and in the grid. Cash positive, still not paying your way, and the partnership notices long before the math does.

Two rules at the edges

What happens when the number is very bad or very good.

If the result is negative

No bonus, and a formal flag. A partner-level PIP follows. You make up the shortfall within 1 to 2 years or you are out. Sometimes the exit is immediate.

If the bonus exceeds ~$750K

The excess vests over 3 years. Roughly one third is paid the same year, the rest over the next two. It can be forfeited on exit unless a payout is negotiated.

The smart read

The numbers that explain how the process actually behaves.

15¢

Your marginal take rate on SCR. Each extra dollar of SCR converts at 40% profitability, is halved into the pool, then multiplied at 75%. Selling $1 earns you 15 cents, in a normal year. This excludes the pool entirely, since your pool draw does not move with your selling. The 16 cents stacks on top of a fixed base of roughly ~$244K of all-in comp for an FI1 and ~$1.46M for an FI5 (pool credits × 75%), paid whether you sell or not. The draw then comes out of the combined total.

~$2M

The survival line. Survival means your own projects cover your own salary, with the pool excluded from both sides of the equation. The test values your generation at 100% of credits, before the 50/50 split, then applies the multiplier. SCR × PVA × multiplier must cover the ~$354K draw. At 40% PVA in a 75% year that is ~$1.18M of SCR. At 30% PVA in a 60% year it is ~$2M, the number partners quote, effectively priced for a weak year. It sits well above the arithmetic floor near $735K where the bonus goes negative, so you can be cash positive and still below the line.

−40%

The multiplier taxes everything. Because pool distributions are credits, a Europe-style 60% year hits your entire comp, pool included. The same 725K credits pay ~$190K of bonus at the 75% baseline, ~$226K in an 80% region, and ~$81K in a 60% one. Geography is a comp decision.

The ladder, explained

Why the FI structure, not your own selling, is where partner wealth is actually built.

~$8.1K

What one FI unit is worth. At ~325K credits for 30 units, each unit is roughly 10.8K credits, or about $8.1K of annual comp at a 75% multiplier. Every promotion is just units, and every unit is an annuity.

+$365K/yr

The money promotion. FI1 to FI2 adds 15 units, worth roughly $120K per year at maturity. FI2 to FI3 adds 45 units, roughly $365K per year. The ladder is back-loaded, and so is the incentive to stay.

10-20 yrs

The glide path makes it slower than it looks. A minimum of ~2 years per bracket, promotions dependent on your sponsoring senior partner, and each step phasing in gradually (30, 35, 40, 45). The system is engineered so seniority compounds slowly and cannot be shortcut by selling.

17¢

The bottom of the ladder funds the top. An FI1 draws 17 cents on the dollar of an FI5's pool share. And because everyone contributes half their PVA regardless of level, junior partners' selling subsidizes senior partners' draws until they climb high enough to be on the receiving side.
