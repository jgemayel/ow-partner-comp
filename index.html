From project to credits

Three steps between the client signing and credits landing in your name. Identical for every partner regardless of seniority.

Step 1 · Splits & SCR

The code holder decides

When a project sells, revenue is split among partners on the code. The code holder proposes, everyone else negotiates. Splits are set at the start and can be revised ex-post. Your share becomes your SCR, Single Count Revenue. $2M of SCR means roughly $4M of sold work at 50% splits.

Step 2 · PVA

Profitability, not revenue

SCR converts to PVA based on project profitability. Around 40% is typical. This is the number that actually feeds your credits.

Step 3 · The 50/50

Half yours, half the pool's

PVA splits evenly. Half stays with you as personal credits. Half funds the partner pool, which redistributes by seniority. Credits are an internal unit, not dollars. Nothing becomes money until the very end.

The detail that changes everything. The pool distributes credits, not dollars. So the year-end multiplier is not a haircut on your personal selling. It taxes your entire compensation, pool draw included. All-in comp equals total credits times the multiplier. The bonus is simply all-in minus what was already paid as draw.

Two rules at the edges

What happens when the number is very bad or very good.

If the result is negative

No bonus, and a formal flag. A partner-level PIP follows. You make up the shortfall within 1 to 2 years or you are out. Sometimes the exit is immediate.

If the bonus exceeds ~$750K

The excess vests over 3 years. Roughly one third is paid the same year, the rest over the next two. It can be forfeited on exit unless a payout is negotiated.

The smart read

The numbers that explain how the process actually behaves.

16¢

Your marginal take rate on SCR. Each extra dollar of SCR converts at 40% profitability, is halved into the pool, then multiplied at 80%. Selling $1 earns you 16 cents, in a good year. This excludes the pool entirely, since your pool draw does not move with your selling. The 16 cents stacks on top of a fixed base of roughly $260K of all-in comp for an FI1 and ~$1.56M for an FI5 (pool credits × 80%), paid whether you sell or not. The draw then comes out of the combined total.

~$590K

The survival line, and it moves. At an 80% multiplier an FI1 needs about $590K of SCR to clear the draw. At a 60% multiplier the same line jumps to roughly $1.33M. The firm's year decides how hard your year has to be.

−40%

The multiplier taxes everything. Because pool distributions are credits, a Europe-style 60% year hits your entire comp, pool included. The same 725K credits pay ~$226K of bonus in an 80% region and ~$81K in a 60% one. Geography is a comp decision.

The ladder, explained

Why the FI structure, not your own selling, is where partner wealth is actually built.

~$8.7K

What one FI unit is worth. At ~325K credits for 30 units, each unit is roughly 10.8K credits, or about $8.7K of annual comp at an 80% multiplier. Every promotion is just units, and every unit is an annuity.

+$390K/yr

The money promotion. FI1 to FI2 adds 15 units, worth roughly $130K per year at maturity. FI2 to FI3 adds 45 units, roughly $390K per year. The ladder is back-loaded, and so is the incentive to stay.

10-20 yrs

The glide path makes it slower than it looks. A minimum of ~2 years per bracket, promotions dependent on your sponsoring senior partner, and each step phasing in gradually (30, 35, 40, 45). The system is engineered so seniority compounds slowly and cannot be shortcut by selling.

17¢

The bottom of the ladder funds the top. An FI1 draws 17 cents on the dollar of an FI5's pool share. And because everyone contributes half their PVA regardless of level, junior partners' selling subsidizes senior partners' draws until they climb high enough to be on the receiving side.

How to read the system

Your own selling is a qualifier, the pool is the payout, and the multiplier is a firm-wide tax on both. Selling keeps your seat. Units make you rich. And units only come with time, sponsorship and patience, by design.
